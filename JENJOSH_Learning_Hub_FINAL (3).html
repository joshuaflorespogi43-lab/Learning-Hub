📖 Learn Zone: Fundamental Movements

Fundamental movement skills are basic movement abilities that help learners participate in games, dance, gymnastics, sports, and everyday activities. They can become building blocks for more complex physical activities.

🚶 Locomotor Movements

Meaning: Locomotor movements are movements that take the body from one place to another.

Examples: walking, running, hopping, jumping, skipping, galloping, sliding, and leaping.

Example: Running around a cone or jumping over an obstacle is locomotor because the body travels through space.

🧍 Non-Locomotor Movements

Meaning: Non-locomotor movements are movements performed mainly without traveling to another location.

Examples: balancing, stretching, twisting, turning, swaying, spinning, bending, and curling.

Example: Standing in one place while twisting the body is mainly non-locomotor.

🏀 Manipulative / Object-Control Skills

Meaning: Manipulative skills are movements used to control or move an object with the hands, feet, or equipment.

Examples: throwing, catching, kicking, striking, dribbling, and punting.

Example: Throwing a beanbag toward a marked target is a manipulative action.

🧠 Easy Memory Trick

LOCO = GO ➡️ (travel) • NON-LOCO = STAY 🧍 (mostly in place) • MANIPULATIVE = OBJECT 🎾.

📍 Personal Space and General Space

Personal space is the space immediately around your body. General space is the larger area shared with others during a movement activity.

🗺️ Movement Concepts

Direction
The way the body moves, such as forward, backward, sideways, or diagonally.
Speed
How quickly or slowly a movement is performed.
Pathway
The route taken by a body or object. Pathways can be straight, curved, or zig-zag.
Levels
The height at which a movement happens, such as low, medium, or high.
Relationships
How a learner moves in relation to people, objects, or body parts.
Space
The area in which movement takes place.

🌟 Why Fundamental Movement Skills Matter

  • They support physical development.
  • They help learners participate in games and physical activities.
  • They provide practice in body control and coordination.
  • They can support social participation through games.
  • They serve as building blocks for more complex activities and sports.

🧩 Foundational Skills

Each foundational skill below includes a meaning, explanation, and example so learners can understand what the concept means and how it can be applied in physical activity.

1Movement Concept – Relationship with People and Object

Movement Concept – Meaning: Movement concepts describe how the body moves and uses space, direction, pathways, levels, and relationships during physical activity.
Relationship with People and Objects – Meaning: This refers to how a learner moves in relation to other people or objects. The learner notices where people and objects are and adjusts movement based on their location.

Example: A learner sees a cone directly ahead, changes direction, and moves around it. The learner is using awareness of the object's location and the movement pathway.

Why it matters: Being aware of people and objects helps learners move safely and make appropriate movement decisions.

2Movement Skills – Locomotor, Manipulative

Locomotor – Meaning: A movement skill that carries the body from one place to another, such as running, hopping, jumping, skipping, galloping, sliding, or leaping.
Manipulative – Meaning: A movement skill used to control or propel an object. Examples include throwing, catching, kicking, striking, dribbling, and punting.

Target-game example: Run around an obstacle and then throw a beanbag toward a marked target. The running is locomotor while the throw is manipulative.

Why it matters: These skills give learners movement tools they can use in games, sports, recreation, and active living.

3Fitness Concepts – Agility, Balance, Coordination, Speed

Agility – Meaning: The ability to change body position or direction efficiently while moving.
Balance – Meaning: The ability to maintain stability and body control while still or moving.
Coordination – Meaning: The ability to make body parts work together smoothly and effectively.
Speed – Meaning: The ability to perform a movement quickly.
Intensity: Moderate to Vigorous Physical Activities (MVPA) – Meaning: MVPA refers to physical activity performed at moderate to vigorous levels of effort. It describes how demanding the activity is for the learner.

Example: Moving quickly through an obstacle course can require agility and speed, while maintaining body control requires balance and coordination.

Why it matters: These fitness concepts help learners understand different physical demands during active movement and games.

4Game Concepts – Positioning and Relationship to Objects

Positioning – Meaning: Choosing and maintaining a useful location in the activity area while being aware of people, objects, space, and the target.
People and Location in Space – Meaning: Knowing where other people are and adjusting movement and position so space can be used safely and effectively.
Relationship to Objects – Meaning: Understanding where your body is in relation to a ball, target, marker, cone, or other object.
Sending Away – Meaning: Propelling an object away from the body toward an intended area.
Throw – Meaning: Sending an object through space using the hand or hands.
Strike – Meaning: Sending an object away by hitting it with a body part or equipment.

Example: A learner checks the target, chooses a safe position, faces the target, and throws a beanbag toward the intended area.

Why it matters: Good positioning and object awareness can help learners make purposeful and safer choices during target games.

🎯 Learning Competencies

The learners perform physical activities using target game concepts with agility, balance, coordination, and speed for active and healthy living:
a. Locomotor skills by avoiding an object or obstacles

Meaning: Learners use traveling movements such as running, hopping, jumping, or changing direction to move safely around or away from an object or obstacle.

Example: A learner runs toward a cone, changes direction, and continues moving without touching it.

b. Manipulative skills by sending or propelling an object to an intended area

Meaning: Learners use skills such as throwing or striking to send an object toward a chosen target or intended area with control and purpose.

Example: A learner throws a beanbag toward a marked circle and tries to control the direction and force of the throw.

⭐ Putting the Competency Together

Agility helps with changing direction around obstacles. Balance helps with body control. Coordination helps body parts work together. Speed helps the learner move quickly when appropriate.

📝 Learning Objectives

At the end of the learning experience, the learners should be able to:

1Explain Fundamental Movement Concepts

Objective: Identify and explain basic movement concepts, including relationships with people, objects, and space.

Meaning: Learners understand how their bodies move in relation to other people, objects, pathways, directions, and available space.

2Perform Locomotor Skills Safely

Objective: Perform locomotor skills such as walking, running, hopping, and jumping while avoiding objects or obstacles.

Meaning: Learners demonstrate control while traveling from one place to another and adjust their movement when an obstacle is present.

3Perform Manipulative Skills

Objective: Demonstrate manipulative skills by sending or propelling an object toward an intended area.

Meaning: Learners use actions such as throwing or striking to control the direction of an object and send it toward a target.

4Apply Fitness Concepts

Objective: Demonstrate agility, balance, coordination, and speed during physical activities.

Meaning: Learners use quick changes of direction, body control, coordinated movements, and appropriate movement speed during activities.

5Apply Target-Game Concepts

Objective: Apply positioning and relationship-to-object concepts when participating in target-game activities.

Meaning: Learners choose useful positions, observe people and objects around them, and adjust their location before sending an object toward an intended area.

6Participate Actively and Safely

Objective: Participate actively, safely, and cooperatively in physical activities for active and healthy living.

Meaning: Learners follow activity rules, become aware of shared space, cooperate with others, and make safe movement choices.

🌟 Goal: Learners will not only know the movement concepts but also apply them through active, purposeful, and enjoyable physical activities.

💡 Fun Facts & Quick Knowledge

🔎 Movement Through History

There is no single person credited with discovering locomotor or non-locomotor movement. Humans have used basic movements throughout history, while physical education systems have developed ways to describe, classify, and teach movement skills.

🛣️ Movement Pathways

Traveling movements can use straight, curved, and zig-zag pathways.

🧱 Movement Building Blocks

Fundamental movement patterns can become building blocks for more complex activities and sports.

🧩 Skills Can Combine

A real activity can combine traveling, balancing, changing direction, and object control in one sequence.

🌟 Physical Literacy

Fundamental movement skills support physical literacy and participation in physical activities.

🎯 Target Games

Target-game situations often require learners to consider positioning, the relationship to an object, and how to send an object toward an intended area.
